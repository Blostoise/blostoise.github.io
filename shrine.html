The Nine Lights of the Shrine

It is said that long ago, in a forest shrine where the autumn leaves never fully fell, there lived a Ninetales who had watched over the grounds for three hundred years. So long had she kept her post that the villagers stopped counting her age and simply called her the Keeper.

They say the spirit who inhabited the old shrine had watched her all those centuries, and had grown to love her for it. Not love as villagers understood it, but the old, patient love of something ancient for something faithful. The spirit had seen countless Pokémon and people come and go beneath the gate. None had ever stayed. None had ever cared for the shrine as she did.

The Keeper's time came at last, and the villagers buried her near the shrine she had guarded so long. The spirit could not bear to see three hundred years of devotion end in silence. It resolved to give her a second chance.

It gathered what she had left behind: the nine tails, bright as embers, the golden shine of her fur, the warmth that had settled into the shrine stones after centuries of her sleeping there. From these, and from the memory of the pilgrims who once climbed the shrine steps, the spirit shaped something new. Two hands. Two legs. A face that could smile, a gift she had never needed before.

This, the stories say, was no punishment and no accident. It was thanks, given the only way a spirit that old knew how to give it.

She woke at the base of the gate not knowing herself. She reached back for tails that were no longer there and found only a long braid of golden hair. When she tried to speak, her voice came out thin and strange, stripped of the old fire that once ran through it. When she wept no sparks fell with her tears.

The next day, the villagers found a woman none of them knew at the shrine; golden-haired, amber-eyed, saying nothing at all. The eldest among them looked at the way she watched that shrine, patient and unblinking, like she was still keeping some old promise. The eldest knew instantly what had happened, but resolved to keep her secret. The superstitious would not be kind to one who came back from death.

Some say she tended the shrine every day for the rest of her life, just as she once guarded it on four legs. Only now, she could talk to visitors, and understand their stories. She had found something in this life that the past had not known; companionship.

She never spoke of what she had been. It is said that on the clearest autumn evenings you could see nine faint lights flicker in the dusk at her back, gone the instant you were sure you'd seen them.

The spirit had given her the greatest reward it knew. A second life, spent beside the place she loved most, in a body built to experience far more than it had before..
